Privacy Policy — Chit: Notes for Places

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Chit ("the app") is a notes app made by [OWNER NAME / CONTACT EMAIL] ("we", "us"). This policy explains what data the app handles, where it goes, and why. It applies to the Chit app (package id com.pixeldreamland.locationnotes) on Android and iOS.

The short version

  • Your notes, saved places, and alerts are stored only on your device. We never see them, and there is no account, sign-in, or cloud sync.
  • The app has no analytics, no advertising, and no crash-reporting SDKs. Nothing about how you use the app is sent anywhere.
  • A few features send small, specific pieces of information to outside services only when you use those features, described below.
  • Background location is used only to detect when you enter or leave a place you've set an alert for, so the app can show you a notification. It is never uploaded anywhere.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in the app — notes, fragments (text, checklists, images, links), saved places, alerts, and settings — is stored in a local database on your device and is never transmitted to us or to any server we control. We do not operate a backend for this app and have no way to see your data.

Private notes can be locked behind your device's own screen lock or biometric unlock (fingerprint/face). This restricts who can open a private note inside the app; it does not encrypt the note's content on disk. Anyone with direct access to the device's file system (for example, a phone backup extracted on a computer) could still potentially read it. Don't treat private notes as a substitute for full-disk or file encryption.

Photos you attach to notes come from your camera or photo library and are stored on your device only; they are never uploaded.

Location

Foreground location is used to sort your notes by distance and to show which ones are "here now." This never leaves your device except as described below.

Background ("Always") location is used only to power alerts you create — a notification when you enter or leave a place, a chain (e.g., "any McDonald's"), or a category (e.g., "any supermarket") you've chosen to be notified about. The app monitors a small number of geofences (invisible virtual boundaries) around the locations your alerts need; it does not continuously track or log your location or route, and it does not send your location to us. If you have no alerts turned on, the app doesn't request or use background location at all.

Google Maps and Google Places

The app uses the Google Maps SDK to display maps, and the Google Places API when you search for a place by name or address (autocomplete) or select a search result (place details).

  • When you view a map, Google's map-tile servers receive requests for the map tiles needed to draw the area you're viewing — standard for any app that shows a Google Map, and handled under Google's own privacy policy.
  • When you type in the place search box, the text you type is sent to Google's Places API to get suggestions; when you pick a result, its place ID is sent to Google to get its address and coordinates. This only happens when you actively search — it is not triggered by simply opening the app.
  • If you save a place found this way, the app periodically (about every 25 days) asks Google to refresh that place's coordinates in the background, so alerts don't rely on data older than Google allows apps to cache. This is limited to places you've saved that an active alert of yours depends on.

See Google's Privacy Policy for how Google handles this data.

Reverse geocoding (turning coordinates into an address)

When you pin a location on the map, the app asks your phone's built-in geocoding service to turn the coordinates into a readable address to show you. This uses the operating system's own location-lookup service (provided by Google on Android and by Apple on iOS, not by us), governed by their respective privacy policies. If the lookup fails, the app just shows "Pinned location" instead.

Brand and category alerts (place data)

If you set an alert for "any branch of a brand" or "any place of a type" (e.g., "any supermarket"), the app needs to know where nearby matching places are. In the published app, this comes from a pre-built, read-only grid of map files ("tiles") we host on GitHub Pages, built in advance from OpenStreetMap data. The app downloads only the tile(s) covering a rough area around you — each tile covers roughly a 20 km × 20 km square — so the host sees only which rough tile files were requested and when, never your exact coordinates. Tile files are cached on your device and reused for about a week before being re-downloaded.

Notifications

Alert notifications ("you've arrived near X") and any other notifications the app shows are generated entirely on your device using your phone's local notification system. The app doesn't use any push-notification service, and no notification content is sent to us or anyone else.

Exporting your data

The app can create a backup file (a .zip containing your notes, places, alerts, and images) when — and only when — you choose to export it. That file is created locally on your device and handed to your phone's normal share sheet, so it goes wherever you choose to send or save it (e.g., a file, an email, cloud storage you pick). We never receive a copy, and nothing is exported automatically or in the background.

What we don't do

  • We don't collect analytics or usage statistics.
  • We don't show ads or use advertising SDKs.
  • We don't use crash-reporting or telemetry services.
  • We don't have user accounts, and we don't sync your data to any server.
  • We don't sell or share your data with third parties, because we don't have it to begin with — it stays on your device.

Children's privacy

The app is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because the app doesn't collect personal information from anyone in the first place (see above), this is naturally the case for all users.

Your choices and control

  • You can deny or revoke location and notification permissions at any time in your device's settings; the app degrades gracefully (notes just won't be sorted by distance, and alerts won't fire).
  • Deleting a note, place, or alert in the app removes it immediately and permanently from your device; there is no server copy to also delete.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of the app's local data, subject to your operating system's normal app-data rules.

Changes to this policy
